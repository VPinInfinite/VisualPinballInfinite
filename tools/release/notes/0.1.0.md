**Visual Pinball Infinite 0.1.0, the first release.** A new pinball engine for your Visual Pinball X tables, with your library, screens, settings and achievements in the same app. Free and open source, for Windows 10 and 11.

## Install

1. Download `VisualPinballInfinite-0.1.0-setup.exe` below.
2. Run it. The installer isn't signed yet, so Windows may say it protected your PC: choose **More info**, then **Run anyway**.
3. It installs for your Windows user, with no administrator rights needed, and opens on first setup: use your VPX folders as they are, or start a new library.

It needs Windows 10 or 11 (64-bit) and a DirectX 12 graphics card. Tables, ROMs and media aren't included: get them from their authors.

## What it does

### Playing tables

- **Visual Pinball X tables, as they are.** It opens `.vpx` tables directly and never changes them. [115 tables are confirmed working](https://visualpinballinfinite.org/resources/tested-tables).
- **VPX's physics, rewritten in Rust.** VPX's own physics code, rewritten line by line with nothing changed and checked bit for bit against Visual Pinball X's own test runs, so tables play the way their authors tuned them: flippers, rubbers, ramps, spinners, the lot. Physics run in fixed 1 ms steps, so the same inputs always give the same game.
- **Table scripts run unchanged.** VPX tables' VBScript runs on libwinevbs, the VBScript engine from Wine, answered by Visual Pinball Infinite's own versions of every object VPX gives scripts.
- **A script editor.** A table's page opens its script in two tabs: its options, the settings its authors left for players, as boxes, switches and lists; and its whole script, in an editor with line numbers and search. Changes are kept in a script file beside the table, which Visual Pinball X reads too, so the table file stays untouched. Check it plays the script first and lists any errors by line, and a table updated later can carry your changed options over.
- **ROM tables.** PinMAME is included and runs each machine's original program, with its sound, its displays and its lamps and coils. AltSound packs replace a machine's sound with better recordings, and a table without one can have a pack made from its own ROM, on its page. Stern's SAM machines (2006 to 2014) and Pinball 2000 send no sound commands for a pack to follow, so AltSound doesn't work for them.
- **VPX's look.** Its lights, including its model of a real bulb's glow and fade, and its light shows. Its materials lit by the room around the table, reflections in the playfield, glass and clear plastics bending what's behind them, bloom around bright lights, shading in corners and creases, the ball's motion blur, its tone mapping and color grading. On average, the tables we test draw within 0.1% of Visual Pinball X's own picture\* ([how it's measured](https://visualpinballinfinite.org/resources/tested-tables)).
- **HDR.** On a screen with Windows' HDR switched on, the picture goes to the screen in HDR, as in VPX.
- **VPX's picture options.** Multisampling, edge smoothing (FXAA, SMAA, NFAA, DLAA and others), sharpening and supersampling, all off by default, as in VPX.
- **Low latency.** Buttons are read on a thread of their own, never held up by drawing, and each press gets its frame at once. The display timing is your choice: fastest, tear-free and fast, the classic vsync, or frame pacing. A frame-rate limit (240 unless you set another) keeps the graphics card from drawing frames no screen can show.
- **What VPX reads beside a table.** The table's own settings file (`<table>.ini`), its camera file (`<table>.pov`), a fixed script (`<table>.vbs`) and its physics settings (`<table>.vpp`), each read as VPX reads it.
- **Nudging and tilt.** The nudge keys push the table as VPX's do. A gamepad's stick nudges as hard as it's pushed, and a cabinet's nudge sensor feels the real cabinet move, smoothed and calibrated as in VPX. The tilt comes from VPX's simulated tilt bob, or by counting nudges as VPX 10.8.0 does.
- **Plungers.** A gamepad's stick, or a cabinet's analog plunger, works as a plunger.
- **Any button, any device.** Every game action goes on any key, mouse button or controller button: keyboards, gamepads, cabinet boards. A gamepad rumbles where VPX's does.
- **A pause menu.** P, or a gamepad's Back button, opens it: resume, each kind of sound's volume, the table's own options and rules, and Quit.
- **Achievements.** Goals to earn as you play, from achievement packs made by a table's author or anyone else: a score, a multiball, an extra ball, the machine's own lamps, switches and coils, or the table's parts hit so many times in a ball, on a chosen ball, in a game or over all your games, even one after another against the clock. Each is announced on the backglass with a sound, which you can turn down or off, and the pause menu shows how far each has come. They count as Hardcore while the table plays as its author made it, and as Softcore otherwise; PLAY's menu plays a table either way on purpose. A table can have several packs, dropped in like any other file, picked from on its page and deleted there. Connect the app to VPIDB with your Discord account, and what you earn can show there, if you choose (below).
- **Sample achievement packs** for ten well-known tables, Attack from Mars, Medieval Madness and Twilight Zone among them, each showing what a pack can do. How every one of their achievements is built is in [Sample achievement packs](https://visualpinballinfinite.org/resources/achievement-packs).
- **A table's physics.** Its author's physics settings beside what plays, changed and saved beside the table as Visual Pinball X's editor writes them, put back in one go, and kept as presets. Visual Pinball X's eight shared physics sets, which some tables use instead of their own, have a page of their own.
- **Screenshots.** F12 saves the windows you choose, and a picture can be taken by itself when you make a high score.
- **Table scripts kept to the table.** A table's script gets what tables need, such as PinMAME, the backglass and its own files, and nothing more. A table can be allowed to reach outside, the internet or other programs, with a setting that's off unless you turn it on.

### Score displays, backglasses and PinUP packs

- **Backglasses** from `.directb2s` files, with their light shows and animations, and a backglass file's score panel on a third screen.
- **The score display (DMD)** in its own window, in the table, or floating over it on one screen. It's drawn in VPX's newer styles, glowing plasma dots behind glass or LEDs, or in the original look. Older machines' segment displays are drawn digit by digit.
- **Color DMDs.** Serum's colorings (`.cRZ`, `.cROMc`) and PIN2DMD's (`.pal` and `.vni`; `.pac` with your own key).
- **Color your own.** Color it… records a machine's score display, by itself first and then as you play, for a coloring program by other people (ColorizingDMD today, PPUC's once its makers release it), which the app installs from its makers' own page the first time you open it, and puts the finished coloring where the player finds it.
- **DMD upscaling.** Smoother, sharper dots for big screens (ScaleFX, MMPX, xBRZ, Super-xBR), off by default as in VPX.
- **Real DMD panels.** ZeDMD, PIN2DMD, PinDMD, Pixelcade and others, through libdmdutil or your own DMD Extensions. Checked with libdmdutil's virtual panel; not yet tried with a real one.
- **FlexDMD and UltraDMD built in.** Original tables' own DMD scenes, drawn by FlexDMD's code rewritten in Rust, and checked dot for dot against the original.
- **PinUP packs, played by Visual Pinball Infinite's own player.** Videos, pictures, sounds and labels on the topper, backglass, DMD and playfield screens, started by the game's events and by pictures on its score display.
- **Music through Windows Media Player**, for the tables that play it that way, without Windows Media Player.

### Sound

- **Table sounds and music** by VPX's rules for volume, pan and pitch, and PinMAME's sound for ROM tables.
- **Two outputs.** The playfield's sounds and the backglass's (music, the machine, PinUP packs) can go to different speakers.
- **Speaker layouts.** Stereo, surround, and the layouts of cabinets with speakers or shakers under the playfield (SSF), with VPX's older and newer mixing.
- **Volumes** in the settings, for each table, and from the keyboard while you play.

### Screens and cabinets

- **One to four screens:** the playfield, landscape or portrait, the backglass, the score display and a topper.
- **VPX's cabinet view.** The screen is treated as a window onto the playfield, fitted from the screen's size and where you stand.
- **Feedback toys (DOF).** Contactors, knockers, shakers, fans, flashers and LED strips, through libdof, including Philips Hue lights and SSF impact sounds. Your DOF Config Tool files are imported in Settings › Cabinet.
- **Cabinet boards.** Pinscape, Open Pinball Device boards, key encoders and gamepads: buttons, plunger and nudge.
- **Cabinet mode.** The app full screen across the cabinet's screens, for choosing and starting tables with the cabinet's buttons.

### Your library

- **Your folders, as they are.** The app works with the folders you already have: VPX's tables, ROMs, backglasses and PinUP packs, and PinUP Popper's media. Nothing needs moving. First setup finds an installed VPX, or makes a new library of its own.
- **Or a folder for each game,** as Visual Pinball X 10.8.1 keeps tables, a game's versions sharing it. The app shows what goes where before anything moves, moves the files, and can move them back.
- **Every table on a card**, with its picture, maker, year and what it holds. You can also see the tables as a list, search them, keep favourites and playlists (your own, or smart ones from the Virtual Pinball Spreadsheet's tags), and hide tables you don't want to see.
- **Smart playlists and your own.** Smart playlists fill themselves (VPin Workshop's tables, Originals, the 1990s, and any of the Spreadsheet's tags you tick, such as SSF or VR), beside Favourites, Top rated, Recently played, Recently added and Needs attention. Make as many playlists of your own as you like.
- **Ratings.** Give a table one to five stars on its page, and sort the library by them or see your top rated. Ratings you already gave in PinUP Popper, VPin Studio, PinballX or PinballY come over by themselves, and the ones you give in the app can go into their lists too, if you want. Shared with VPIDB if you choose, they help others see which tables are popular.
- **Matched to the Virtual Pinball Spreadsheet.** Each table is found by its name, maker, year, version and authors, for its picture, facts and designers, or chosen by hand when it isn't sure. A table can be renamed to its Spreadsheet name for every program at once, and put back.
- **A page for each table:** its pictures and videos, its facts, PLAY, its achievements and high scores, a log of your plays, what it holds, and every file the Spreadsheet lists for it. Left and right go through your tables.
- **High scores** read from a ROM machine's own memory and from the values a table's script keeps, with Visual Pinball X's beside them.
- **Drop downloads in.** Files, folders, zips, 7-Zip and RAR archives: each file is told apart, matched to its table and shown where it'll go before anything is copied. Drop them on the window, or on a table's page or tile; achievement packs, physics files and finished colorings go in the same way. ROMs are checked file by file against what PinMAME expects, music goes where the table's script plays it from, and anything replaced goes to the Recycle Bin.
- **Files fitted to their tables.** A PinUP pack goes in under the name the table's script asks for, and colorings and AltSound packs made for another version of the game go where they'll play. A file that's there already is replaced (the old one to the Recycle Bin), kept beside it, added to, or left as it is: your choice.
- **Several backglasses for a table.** All of them are kept, and its page chooses which one plays, from a picture of each. Visual Pinball X and VPin Studio see the same files.
- **A table's alternative pictures.** A picture a download brings to replace one of the table's own, such as a different desktop backdrop, is kept beside the table and played in that picture's place. The table file itself never changes.
- **Files swapped, the table file untouched.** Which of its backglasses plays, a picture of its own in place of one of the table's, a changed script, physics of its own, another ROM or another layout of its PinUP pack: each kept beside the table, and each can be put back.
- **A health check.** Every table's ROM is checked file by file, and what a table needs and lacks is marked for attention.
- **Change a table's ROM** to another version of its game, always undoable.
- **VPUniverse patches.** A table mod shared as a `.dif` patch is made from the untouched original.
- **Removing a table or its files**, to the Recycle Bin, each file chosen.
- **Media made for you.** Where a table has none, the engine draws its playfield, seen from straight above, its backglass and score display, records a 20-second preview video of a game starting, with the table's sound, and draws a wheel logo. It can put them where PinUP Popper, PinballX and PinballY look, too.
- **Tools for every table in one place:** ROMs, PinUP packs, media, color DMDs and AltSound packs, each page with a search box.
- **ROMs.** Every set your library has or its tables ask for, with the tables that play it and each file checked. A set under a name PinMAME doesn't look for is renamed, a copy is removed, and a set no table plays can go, each when you say so.
- **PinUP packs managed.** Every pack is listed with its tables, and its layout is chosen for your screens from the pack's own options. A pack is turned on or off for each table.
- **The Player hub:** every table's high scores in one list, your achievements in Hardcore and Softcore (each pack opening to show what you've earned), and your stats: your plays, time played, the most played tables, your latest scores, each week's play, and your library by decade and by maker.
- **A table inspector,** the first part of a table editor: any table seen from above, drawn from its own parts. Click a part to see everything it's set to, and what an achievement can count of it. The table never changes.
- **An achievements editor,** inside the table inspector: make a table's achievements by clicking its own parts on the playfield, or picking them from its list of parts, and saying what must happen: a ramp made 10 times in one ball, a score on ball 1, three bumpers hit in order inside 60 seconds, 30 bumper hits unless the ball goes down an outlane. It offers only what the table can tell, names the machine's coils as the table's script does, and keeps your work as a draft until you finish the pack. Try it by playing, then finish it as the pack's next version.
- **A VPS browser.** Every machine the Virtual Pinball Spreadsheet lists, filtered by maker, era, type and table format, or to the ones you don't have yet, each with every release and file the Spreadsheet links to.

### Settings

- **One place for every setting.** Screens, Picture, Sound, Controls, ROM machines, physics sets, PinUP packs, Cabinet, Library and General, on plain pages. Every one of VPX's own settings is on the Advanced page.
- **Screens.** Each window is dragged where it goes, on a map of your screens or on the screens themselves. There are ready-made layouts and presets, and screens of its own for any table. Cabinet mode can have a preset of its own.
- **Controls** with a live test of every button, stick and plunger.
- **A table's own settings,** over your usual ones: its script's options, its view and screens, its look (tone mapper, exposure, scene lighting and difficulty), its volumes, its packs on or off, its physics, and its machine's settings (its sound and volume, its start-up tests, cheat mode).
- **Light from where you live.** A table's room light can follow the sun where you are.
- **Plain text.** The settings are kept in `Settings.ini` under the names VPX uses, so they can be read, shared and backed up. [Every setting](https://visualpinballinfinite.org/resources/settings) is listed with what it does.

### Starting, updating and reporting

- **One installer.** No Visual Pinball X, PinMAME or anything else to install first, and no administrator rights needed. You bring your own tables and ROMs.
- **Front ends.** A front end starts it as it starts Visual Pinball X, with only the program changed. It's checked with PinUP Popper; PinballX and PinballY are set up the same way ([how](https://visualpinballinfinite.org/resources/front-ends)).
- **Updates.** The app finds new versions, checks they're really ours, and installs them when you say so.
- **Report a problem.** It gathers the logs and the facts that help into one file you can look through first, and sends it only if you choose to.
- **Private.** No account and no ads. The app goes online only for the Virtual Pinball Spreadsheet's list and pictures, to look for updates, to send a problem report when you choose to, and, once you connect it to VPIDB, to share what you choose there: your achievements, and your ratings and which tables you have (which table and version only, never file names or folders), so VPIDB can show how many people have each table and its average rating.
- **Free and open source,** under the GNU General Public License, version 3 or later, with every project it builds on credited. It will never be sold.

## VPIDB

[VPIDB](https://vpidb.org), a free site for virtual pinball and a project of its own, is where achievements are shared and achievement packs and parts are found.

- **Your achievements there:** link the app to VPIDB with your Discord account (**VPIDB** at the top of the app), and what you earn is shared as you play. Hardcore achievements count toward masteries, and each pack's page shows who mastered it; Softcore shows only on your own page. Linking also shares which tables you have and your ratings, unless you switch that off; others only ever see totals. Turn sharing off whenever you like, and hide or delete what you shared from your page.
- **Achievement packs:** a table's page in the app adds VPIDB's packs for its game in one click, and every pack can be downloaded from its page on VPIDB with no account. The [Achievements](https://vpidb.org/#/achievements) tab lists them all, labelled Official, Community, Sample or Mod.
- **Parts for table builders:** VPIDB's [Parts](https://vpidb.org/#/parts) library, for 3D models, textures, sounds, scripts and lights, free for anyone to use in their own tables, with no account needed to download.
- **For table authors:** [apply](https://vpidb.org/#/apply) to become an approved author, to host your own releases, share parts and send achievement packs (Official when VPS credits you on every table a pack is for). Every upload is checked by hand before it goes live.

## Good to know

- A ROM machine that has never run on your PC gets a first start of its own before its first game, about 15 seconds, once.
- Cabinet hardware (toys, cabinet boards and real DMD panels) is built and checked against the libraries the hardware uses, but hasn't been tried in a real cabinet yet: tell us how it goes.
- Something wrong? Use **Report a problem** in the app, ask on [Discord](https://discord.gg/GyZTZtsJag), or open an issue here.
- [The wiki](https://github.com/VPinInfinite/VisualPinballInfinite/wiki) walks through every part of the app, and [every setting](https://visualpinballinfinite.org/resources/settings) is listed on the website.
