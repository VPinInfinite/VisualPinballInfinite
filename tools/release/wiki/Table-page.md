# A table's page

Click a table in the library to open its page. The arrow keys, or a controller's LB and RB, move to the previous and next table; Esc goes back to the library.

## PLAY

**PLAY** starts the table in VPX, with its backglass, score display and PinUP pack where Settings › Screens puts them. The app steps aside once VPX's window shows, and comes back when the game ends, on the same table. While it plays in a [newer VPX 10.8.1 build](Installing-and-first-setup#which-vpx-1081-build), F12 or a controller's menu button opens the app's [pause menu](Playing#the-pause-menu) (Settings › Controls › **The pause menu** can give it back to VPX's own). With VPX 10.8.1 in your library, a ROM machine VPX has never run is started once first, out of sight, so it restores its factory settings before your game rather than in it; a notice says so. See [Playing](Playing).

For a table with achievements, PLAY says whether it plays **Hardcore**, as its author made it, or **Softcore**, and why (point at it to see). Its **▾** plays one game either way on purpose: **Play Hardcore** leaves your table settings, script edits and physics changes out for that game (your screens, sound and controls stay yours), and **Play Softcore** keeps its achievements as softcore, for a guest, say. See [Achievements](Achievements#hardcore-and-softcore).

## The VPX it plays with

Tables play in the VPX Settings › Library chooses: VPX 10.8.1, or VPX 10.8.0 where both are in your library (see [Settings](Settings#library)). To play one table in VPX 10.8.0 while the rest play in 10.8.1, choose **Play it with VPX 10.8.0** in its **…** menu. Its achievements then count once each game is over, as VPX 10.8.0 takes no add-ons. Its page then says **Plays with VPX 10.8.0, for this table alone**, with **Play it with Settings › Library's VPX again**.

The same choice is offered where a table stops in VPX 10.8.1: on its test, and in the notes after a game. Each newer VPX 10.8.1 build your library gets tries such a table again by itself, out of sight. Once one plays there, its page says **It plays in VPX 10.8.1 now**: **Play it with VPX 10.8.1**, or **Keep VPX 10.8.0** until a newer build tries it again. See [Playing](Playing#which-vpx-plays-it).

## After a game

When you come back, a notice names the achievements the game earned. If VPX stopped with a problem, a notice offers **Report it**, with VPX's log in the report.

What VPX's log says of the game that you can do something about shows on the table's page under **From your last game**, until you **Dismiss** it:

- **VPX ran short of graphics memory:** it skipped some pictures it needed, which shows as a pink or empty picture. Where supersampling is above 1×, **Set supersampling to 1×** frees a lot of memory; **Open Settings › Picture** has the rest.
- **Its script stopped with an error:** the line and what VPX said, with **Play it with VPX 10.8.0** where VPX 10.8.0 is in your library, and **Report it**.
- **VPX couldn't find the screen "…":** which screen VPX used instead, with **Open Settings › Screens**. Where the settings named the screen the way older VPX builds did, the app names it as your VPX needs and marks it **Fixed**: it's right from the next game.

See [Playing](Playing#after-a-game).

## Its pictures and videos

The big picture shows its playfield video or picture; the arrows on it step through all its media. Below it, a stack for each kind (playfield, backglass, score display, topper, wheel, flyer, instruction card and the rest) holds what's there: VPS's pictures, your front end's media for it (PinUP Popper's, PinballX's or PinballY's), media the app drew itself (marked **drawn by the app**), and **Its media**, the pictures and videos that came with the table for no screen in particular (see [Media](Media#a-tables-own-media)). **Screenshots** holds the screenshots taken while you played it. An empty square stands for each kind it hasn't got yet: click it to make it, or to find VPS's files of that kind.

- **Choosing the one shown.** Open a stack and pick **Show this one**: the one with the blue check shows on its tile, its page and in cabinet mode. It's for browsing only: it doesn't change the table, or what it shows when you play. **PinUP Popper shows this one** marks the one in Popper's own folder (and the same for PinballX and PinballY). **Put it in PinUP Popper's folder** puts yours there: see [Media](Media#media-for-pinup-popper).
- **Right-click** a picture or video to turn it a quarter right or left, or **Show it as its file has it**. Turning only changes how the app shows it; the file stays as it is. The same menu offers **Hide it from this page** for VPS's pictures, or **Show the file** in its folder and **Delete…** to the Recycle Bin for the rest. VPS's pictures can only be hidden; **Show them again** brings them back.
- **Sound:** a video with sound plays muted; the speaker at its bottom right turns the sound on, for every video until the app closes.
- **Make media** (the film button beside PLAY) has the app draw its playfield, backglass and score display, a preview video and a wheel, with no window: see [Media](Media#making-media).

## Its facts

Its name, maker, year, type, version and authors, from VPS, with its players, board, designers, theme, ROM and the features of this release, and links **On VPS** and **On IPDB**. A line says whether it was found on VPS (**Not the right one?** beside it) or isn't on VPS, its facts then coming from its own file (**Find it on VPS**). When its VPS match is only a guess, the page asks **Is this …?** first: see [Your library](Your-library#matching-tables-to-vps).

**Its rating:** the five stars under its version and authors. Click a star to give the table that many, and click the same star again to clear it. Beside them: **Your rating**, **Not rated yet**, or **Its rating in PinUP Popper** (or PinballX, or PinballY) for stars you gave it there. See [Your library](Your-library#ratings).

## What it holds

Every file it plays with, each checked: its table file, backglass, ROM, PinUP pack, AltSound pack, color DMD, POV, physics, settings, script and achievement packs.

- **Its builds:** where the library shows a table with several table files once, a tab for each sits at the top: see [Your library](Your-library#a-table-with-several-table-files). A table file saved twice is asked about here too.
- **Show** opens a file's folder. For something missing, **Find** looks for it among VPS's files.
- **The backglass row:** its file, how many more are kept beside it with **Change…**, and **Score display…**: see [Its backglass](#its-backglass).
- **The ROM row:** its check, file by file, and **Change** to choose another ROM. See [ROM machines](ROM-machines).
- **The PinUP pack row:** its layout, **Layout…** and an **Enable** switch that turns the pack off for this table alone. A pack there under a name its script doesn't ask for says **Named wrong**, and **Fix** renames it (**Rename it**). A pack renamed for this table has **Undo rename**. See [PinUP packs](PinUP-packs).
- **The AltSound row:** its pack's check, and **Listen** for a pack made from its ROM. For a machine without one, **Make one from its ROM…** See [AltSound](AltSound).
- **The Color DMD row:** its coloring, and **Color it…** to color the machine's display yourself. See [Score displays](Score-displays#color-dmds).
- **The Achievements rows:** each of its packs, with how many you've earned of it, or **Add a pack…** where it has none. Its bin deletes the pack for good, after asking. See [Achievements](Achievements#deleting-a-pack).
- **The bin** on a row moves that one file to the Recycle Bin, after asking, and says what that means first: the game won't start without its ROM, say, or another table uses it too.
- **Other:** what else it has. That's the music its script plays (and the songs it plays that aren't there), **Its sounds** that came with it, each with **Listen**, scripts of its own, the folders its DMD pictures come from, the pictures kept for it (see [Your pictures in a copy of the table](#your-pictures-in-a-copy-of-the-table)), other files beside it named for it, and what the drop window added for it. A read-me or manual has **Read**: see [Read-mes and manuals](#read-mes-and-manuals).
- **More on VPS:** everything VPS lists for it, kind by kind, once its VPS match is confirmed. Each file's **Get** opens its page inside the app, beside the table's: see [A file's page inside the app](Your-library#a-files-page-inside-the-app).

To add files for a table, drop them anywhere on its page: see [Adding tables](Adding-tables).

## Achievements, high scores and plays

One card holds its **Achievements** and its **High scores**: click either at its top.

- **Achievements:** its pack's achievements, those you've earned lit up with the day you earned them, as a grid or a list, with how many you've earned and their points. Where a table has several packs, choose which one shows. Where it plays softcore, the card says why, and an achievement VPX can't follow on this table says so. See [Achievements](Achievements#on-a-tables-page). The trophy button beside PLAY jumps to it.
- **High scores:** **Yours** lists your best games, from its play log. **All** lists the scores the table keeps, as VPX keeps them: a ROM machine's are read from its memory, through the Pinball Memory Maps, and a table's own come from what its script saves. Scores an earlier version of the app kept for a table go along to VPX at its first game there, where VPX keeps none of its own.
- **Play log:** each time you played it, for how long, and its last game's scores where they're known.
- **Your plays:** under its links, when you last played it, how many times, for how long in all, and your best score.

## Read-mes and manuals

A read-me or manual kept beside the table and named for it, such as a PDF manual the drop window put there, has **Read** on its row under **Other**. It opens in the app: a PDF in the app's own viewer, a text file as text, whatever it was written in. The pause menu's **Rules** shows them too while you play: see [Playing](Playing).

## Its settings

**Table settings** (the sliders button beside PLAY) holds the settings for this table alone, over your usual ones:

- **Its own options:** the options its author built into its script.
- **On VPS:** its VPS match, with **Change…**. A table renamed to VPS's name has **Put the names back**.
- **View:** your usual, **Desktop**, **Cabinet** or **Full single screen**. **Custom…** opens the Screens page for this table alone (see [Screens](Screens#a-tables-own-screens)).
- **Its screens:** your usual screens, or one of your screen presets.
- **Playfield volume** and **Backglass volume.**
- **Tone mapper**, **Exposure**, **Scene lighting** (the table's own, a level of your own, or by the time of day) and **Difficulty:** VPX's own settings for each table. The difficulty is how steep the table is, how big the flippers and how much the ball scatters.
- **AltSound pack**, **Color DMD** and **PinUP pack:** each as you usually have it, on or off.
- **Screenshot at a high score:** as you usually have it, on or off.
- **Physics:** the table's own, or the physics file beside it that plays (**Show**), and **Change…**: see [Its physics](#its-physics). A `.vpp` dropped on the table goes beside it.
- **The machine,** where the classic VPinMAME plays a ROM table's machine (VPX's own PinMAME keeps no settings for each game): the classic VPinMAME's settings for its game, shared by every table that plays it: its sound, its volume, older machines' recorded sounds, skipping its start-up tests (PinMAME's cheat mode), and, with a display device beside VPinMAME, whether it shows on the real DMD and its color there (see [ROM machines](ROM-machines#its-machines-settings)). Where its script sets one of them each time it starts, the window says so.

**Save** keeps them beside the table in `<table>.ini`, which VPX reads too. The machine's are kept where the classic VPinMAME keeps each game's, in your part of Windows' registry.

## Its own screen settings

VPX's own menu, during a game, can save a change for one table alone, in that table's own settings file. When that file sets windows of its own, VPX uses them for the table instead of your screens, and its page says **This table has its own screen settings**, each window for this table beside yours:

- **Use my screens** takes its window settings out of its file, keeping the rest, so your screens apply.
- **Keep this table's** keeps them, and the page says no more until they change.

The drop window asks the same when a table comes with a settings file that sets its windows: see [Adding tables](Adding-tables#how-the-app-sorts-it-out).

## Its backglass

When a table has more than one backglass, its **Backglass** row says how many more are kept, and **Change…** shows a picture of each. **Play this one** makes it the one that plays; **Play none** keeps them all beside the table and plays none (a table with a PinUP pack shows that instead).

**The score display on the backglass.** [Newer VPX 10.8.1 builds](Installing-and-first-setup#which-vpx-1081-build) keep the score display's place in each backglass file. With those, while Settings › Screens › **The score display in it** is **Found in its picture**, the app places it by itself in every backglass made for a DMD that has a window for it, keeping each file as it came first. **Score display…** on the Backglass row places it by hand: drag the box onto the window in the backglass picture and its corner to size it (or type its **Left**, **Top**, **Width** and **Height**), with **Find it in the picture** and **Undo changes** to help. **Save** writes it into the backglass file, as VPX's own menu does: VPX shows it there from the table's next game, and the app leaves a place you set as it is. See [Score displays](Score-displays#over-the-backglass).

## Your pictures in a copy of the table

VPX plays a table's own pictures. Pictures of your own for a table, each named as one of its pictures (a different desktop backdrop, say), are kept beside it in a `<table> pictures` folder: the drop window offers them (see [Adding tables](Adding-tables#how-the-app-sorts-it-out)). Their row under **Other** has **Make the copy**: the app writes a copy of the table beside it, `<table> (alt pictures).vpx`, with your pictures in place and the table's own backglass, settings, POV, script and physics copied under its name, and makes it one of the table's builds. **Open the copy** opens its page; **Make it again** makes it afresh, the copy before going to the Recycle Bin. A picture named as no picture of the table is left out, and the notice says so. The table itself never changes.

## Its script

**Edit its script** (the `<>` button) opens a table's script in two tabs:

- **Its options:** the settings its authors left near the top of its script for players to change, each with what its comment says, as a box, a switch or a list of choices. Its ROM is there too, with **Change…**, and **Open its settings** where it has an options menu of its own.
- **Its whole script:** every line, colored, with line numbers, **Find** (Ctrl+F) and **Go to line**.

Both tabs show the same script: change an option and its line changes in the whole script. **Save** keeps it beside the table as `<table>.vbs`, which VPX plays in place of the table's own script; the table file itself never changes. **Check it** plays the script as you have it in VPX for a few seconds, unseen and unheard, and lists any errors by line; click one to go there. **Open in Notepad** opens the same file there. **Put back the table's own script** sends the one beside it to the Recycle Bin.

When a table you changed is updated (its file replaced by a newer one), its page asks whether to carry your option changes over to its new script: **Carry them over**, or **Play its new script as it is**.

## Its physics

**Change…** on the Physics row of its settings opens its physics window. It shows the values its author set beside the ones that play: for the table (gravity, friction, bounciness, scatter, and its slope at the easiest and hardest difficulty) and for its flippers (mass, strength, bounciness, scatter, how they hold at the end of their stroke, how fast they fall back, friction, and how fast the coil comes to full strength). A value that differs from its author's is marked.

- **Save beside it** keeps yours as the table's own physics file (`<table>.vpp`), which VPX reads from the next game on. The table file stays as it is.
- **Back to its author's** sends that file to the Recycle Bin; **Fill in its author's** puts its values back in the boxes.
- **Presets:** **Keep these as a preset…** keeps the values under a name, to **Use** on any table. **Bring one in…** adds a physics file from elsewhere, one shared or exported from VPX's editor.

The window says when the table plays `autovpp.vpp`, the physics file for every table in its folder without one of its own, or one of VPX's physics sets from your settings, over everything else (see [Settings](Settings#physics-sets)). A table without flippers has no physics of its own to change. Achievements earned with changed physics count as softcore (see [Achievements](Achievements#hardcore-and-softcore)).

## Test it

**Test it** runs the table in VPX for about 10 seconds, unseen and unheard: its script, and its machine for a ROM table. Its page then says how it went: it plays in VPX, its script stopped with an error (and where), its machine didn't start, or VPX or the table showed a message as it started. **Test again** runs it again. Where its script stopped in VPX 10.8.1 and VPX 10.8.0 is in your library, **Play it with VPX 10.8.0** is offered beside it. Tests run in VPX 10.8.1, so it needs to be in your library (Settings › Library gets it).

## The buttons beside PLAY

**Favourite** (the heart), **Edit its details**, **Table settings**, **Edit its script**, **Show its files**, **Make media**, **Its achievements** (the trophy), and **…**, which holds **Add to a playlist…**, **Open on VPS's site**, **Make media…**, **Open in the table inspector**, **Show VPX's last log**, **Play it with VPX 10.8.0** (or **Play it with Settings › Library's VPX**), **Report a problem with it…**, **Copy where its file is** and **Remove from library…**. Its screenshots open from its pictures, under **Screenshots**.
